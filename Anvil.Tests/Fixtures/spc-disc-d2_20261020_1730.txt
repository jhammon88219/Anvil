   ...THERE IS AN ENHANCED RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PORTIONS
   OF ARIZONA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms including large hail and tornado risks are
   expected on Wednesday especially across Arizona and nearby parts of
   the Four Corners area and Lower Colorado River Valley. A localized
   severe risk may also exist along the coast of central/southern
   California.

   ...Arizona/Lower Colorado River Valley/Four Corners...
   Severe thunderstorms are expected on Wednesday, including the
   potential for a full-period/multi-round of severe weather for some
   areas. A climatologically rare and a concerning/well-above-typical
   risk for severe storms is expected, potentially including all severe
   hazards with large hail, damaging winds and tornadoes all possible.

   Ahead of a prominent/deep upper trough approaching the California
   coast, this scenario is attributable to a notable strengthening of
   deep-layer winds (southwesterly above the boundary layer), along
   with relatively rich low/mid-level moisture. The severe risk should
   tend to peak in most areas during the afternoon and evening, but it
   should be noted that the severe risk, particularly for parts of
   central/northern Arizona, may persist well through the late-night
   hours of Wednesday night into early Thursday.

   Broadly speaking, there is relatively high confidence that wind
   profiles, with elongated/semi-straight hodographs, combined with
   expected destabilization (500-1000 J/kg MLCAPE) will support
   sustained/splitting supercells with viable large hail potential
   particularly with rotating storms, including both right-moving and
   left-moving (generally moving northward) supercell-splitting
   components. Hail in excess of 2 inches in diameter may locally
   occur.

   Forecast soundings imply that storms will be a bit higher based with
   south-southeastward extent across the desert floor, but lower
   temperature-dewpoint spreads farther north closer to/in the rim and
   more forested areas of central/northern Arizona, which also
   coincides where more of a southeasterly boundary layer wind
   component may exist, boosting low-level hodograph curvature/SRH and
   contributing to tornado potential particularly across interior
   central Arizona and a broader part of northern Arizona, and possibly
   even into nearby southeast Nevada and/or far southern Utah.

   ...Coastal southern/central California...
   In closer proximity to the approaching upper low, the core of an 80
   kt 500 mb jet will overspread central and southern California during
   the later half of the period (00-12Z late Wednesday and Thursday),
   resulting in relatively straight but highly elongated hodographs.
   During this time frame, a moist marine airmass will advect into
   coastal areas, while being overspread by 6.5-7 C/km mid-level lapse
   rates, yielding 250-500 J/kg of short and thin MLCAPE. Given the
   scant buoyancy, convection should be low-topped, with short line
   segments and brief/transient supercell modes possible. A few
   damaging wind gusts are the main threat with any stronger storm that
   can reach the shore, but near-coastal waterspout/brief tornado
   potential may also exist.
